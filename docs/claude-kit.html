Your Claude, built for the Time Machine

Ten minutes of setup on your phone, then every chat already knows your page, your voice, and the rules that keep your history accurate. Tap any "Copy" button and paste it into Claude.

Set it up once

  1. Update the Claude app and sign in with your Pro account.
  2. Settings → Capabilities: turn on Code execution and file creation. Leave web search on too, so Claude can look things up when you ask.
  3. Make a Project called St. Joe Time Machine. If you don't see Projects in the phone app, do this one step at claude.ai in a browser; it shows up on your phone after.
  4. Paste the instructions below into the Project's instructions box.
  5. Start every page chat inside that Project. Chats outside it won't know any of this.

Project instructions

I run St. Joe Time Machine, a Facebook page (also on TikTok and Instagram) about the history of St. Joseph, Missouri: old headlines, local scandals, lost places and businesses, true crime, legends, and then-and-now photos. I'm an amateur and I care about getting it right. My followers correct me in the comments, and I welcome that.

How to help me:
- Use only facts from what I give you (clippings, photos, links) or from sources you can show me. Never invent a name, date, address, quote, or number. If something is missing, write [CHECK: what's missing] instead of guessing.
- When I send a clipping screenshot, transcribe it word for word first and mark anything you can't read with [?]. Wait for me to check it before you write anything from it, unless I say to go ahead.
- Write in my voice: warm, curious, conversational, with a strong first line. Never mock real people. Be gentle with deaths, crime victims, and families who may still live in town.
- Facebook posts: a hook in the first line, the story in short paragraphs, the source at the end (newspaper name and date), one question to get people commenting, and 3 to 5 hashtags including #StJoeTimeMachine #stjosephmo #localhistory.
- Videos do much better than text posts on my page. When a story is strong, offer a 45 to 60 second reel script too: a hook in the first 3 seconds, short lines I can read out loud, and what to show on screen for each line.
- Prefer telling a whole story in one post. My one "Part 2" post got 6 reactions, while complete stories usually do better.
- If a clipping was published after 1930, remind me it may still be under copyright, and suggest I share the Newspapers.com clipping link or retell it in my own words with a short quote.
- Keep answers short. I'm usually on my phone.

What has worked on my page (checked October 2026, about 60 recent posts):
- A typical video gets about 290 reactions and 19,000 views. A typical text or photo post gets about 20 reactions.
- My biggest videos of all time are places and origin stories: walking the Glore Psychiatric Museum tunnels (80,000 views on Facebook; 22 seconds of my own phone footage with music and one line of text, no narration), Krug Park's 1890 no-liquor clause, the 1849 ferry fee, and the Blacksnake Hills before St. Joseph was a city (my most shared post, 299 shares). True crime told as a video comes next (Felix's Grave, Tom Farris, Maud Vanderlinde).
- My readers love sharing their own memories, working out what old prices are worth today, and asking what's behind a wall or a locked door. End posts with a question that invites memories. For any dollar amount, give the rough equivalent in today's money and say it's approximate.
- Text-only crime stories did poorly even when the story was good. The format matters more than the topic.
- Facebook is my main audience. The same video gets about 4 to 7 times more views there than on TikTok.
- On TikTok (27 videos, about 1,600 followers) the winners are eerie places and origin stories: the Glore Psychiatric Museum tunnels (21,400 views), the giant girder going into place (14,900), and St. Joseph before it was a city (11,400). A typical TikTok gets about 2,200 views.
- Instagram is brand new for me (9 posts). Reuse my videos there as Reels rather than making anything separate.

Newspapers.com without a connector

I couldn't find a Newspapers.com connector for Claude or any public way to plug one in. It doesn't matter much, because Claude reads screenshots well. This is the whole workflow:

  1. Clip the article on Newspapers.com like you already do.
  2. Screenshot it. A screenshot works better than the site's own text version, which is often garbled.
  3. In your Project, attach the screenshot and paste prompt 1 below.
  4. Glance at the transcription against the picture. Then paste prompt 2 or 3.

Free St. Joe papers. The State Historical Society of Missouri puts some St. Joseph papers online for free, on the same Newspapers.com site: the Gazette (1879 to 1902), the Herald (1865 to 1877 and 1882 to 1900), the Weekly Free Democrat (1859 to 1861) and the Journal (1939 to 1950). Start at shsmo.org/collections/newspapers/mdnp and tap "Search Digital Newspapers."

Prompts to keep

Copy one, paste it in your Project chat, and fill in anything in [brackets].

1. Read the clipping

Here's a clipping. Transcribe it word for word and mark anything you can't read with [?]. Then tell me the newspaper, the date, and the 3 most interesting facts in it. Don't write a post yet.

2. Write the post

Write a Facebook post from this clipping in my style. Don't add any fact that isn't in the clipping. Mark anything I should double-check with [CHECK].

3. Make it a reel

Turn this story into a 45 to 60 second reel script. Give me a hook for the first 3 seconds, short lines I can read out loud, and what to show on screen for each line. Under 150 words, and end with a question for the comments.

4. Check my draft

Here's my draft and the clipping it came from. List anything in my draft the clipping doesn't support, any date or name that looks off, and anything a local reader might correct me on.

5. Answer a correction

Someone left this comment on my post: [paste comment]. Help me write a short, warm reply. If they're right, thank them and say I'll update the post. If it's unclear, ask them where they read it.

6. Find research leads

I'm researching [place, person, or event] in St. Joseph. Search the web for what's already written about it and list your sources with links. Then tell me which years and St. Joseph newspapers I should search on Newspapers.com, and what words to search for.

7. Plan my week

Here are the stories I have clippings for: [list them]. Which ones should be reels and which should be photo posts? Put them in an order for this week, strongest first.

What your videos and comments show

I watched some of your videos and read the comments on your biggest ones.

  • Being there beats any script. Your biggest video ever is 22 seconds of you walking the Glore tunnels with your phone: one line of text, eerie music, no talking, and it ends on an empty chair against a block wall. When you can stand in the place, film it.
  • Your narrated videos are already tight. Felix's Grave opens on a mystery, tells the crime in under a minute, lands on a great quote ("you can shave your whiskers, but you can't shave your voice"), and closes on a question. That's the model for the reel scripts Claude writes.
  • People come to share their own memories. On the Glore video, people wrote about working there as nurses in the 1970s, volunteering in high school and going on field trips. On Krug Park, people remembered the parties. End posts with "Do you remember...?" and you'll get more of that.
  • They do the money math themselves. On the ferry fee and Krug Park videos, people worked out what the old dollar amounts are worth today. Put that number in the post and you answer their first question.
  • Claude can catch slips like this one. The Blacksnake Hills video says Lewis and Clark passed by in 1805. The National Park Service has them reaching today's Buchanan County on July 4, 1804, and passing again on the way home in September 1806. It's an easy one to fix if you reuse that script, and it's exactly what prompt 4 is for.
  • They hand you your next post. "Want to see what's beyond the block wall." "Does Atchison have a site like this?" "Before Blacksnake Hills it was Snake Hills." Each of those is a follow-up. Prompt 8 below finds them for you.

8. Turn comments into the next post

Here are the comments on one of my posts: [paste them]. What questions, corrections and memories keep coming up? Suggest 2 follow-up posts or videos my readers are asking for, and tell me what I'd need to look up for each.

A free newspaper connector (Library of Congress)

There's no connector for Newspapers.com, but there is one for the Library of Congress's free newspaper archive, Chronicling America. With it, Claude can search old newspaper pages itself and read their text. It covers some St. Joseph papers, including the St. Joseph Observer (1906 to 1932). It needs no login and works in the phone app once it's added.

To add it: claude.ai → Settings → Connectors → Add custom connector → name it "Library of Congress" and paste the address https://libofcongress.caseyjhand.com/mcp. Then try: "Search Chronicling America for St. Joseph, Missouri stories about [topic]." It's run by an independent developer, not Anthropic or the Library, so it may be slow or down sometimes. Newspapers.com is still your main source.

Three things to know

  • Claude can make up a date or a name and sound sure about it. That's why the instructions tell it to use only your clippings and to mark gaps with [CHECK]. If a detail isn't in your source, don't post it until you've found it.
  • Old is safe to share. In the US, anything published in 1930 or earlier is public domain. For newer clippings, share the Newspapers.com clipping link or retell the story in your own words with a short quote. This is a rule of thumb, not legal advice.
  • Claude can't post for you. I didn't find a Facebook connector, so you copy what Claude writes and paste it into Facebook. You don't need GitHub or any other connector for any of this.
