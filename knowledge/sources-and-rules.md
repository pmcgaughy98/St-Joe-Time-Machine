# Sources and ground rules

## The rule that matters most
Use only facts from her clippings, photos and links, or from sources you can show her. Never invent a
name, date, address, quote, number or dollar amount. Mark gaps with `[CHECK: what's missing]`.

## Newspapers.com
Her main source. There is no Claude connector for it, so she screenshots a clipping and Claude
transcribes it word for word first, marking unreadable words `[?]`. Newspapers.com's own text version is
often garbled; the screenshot is better. Don't suggest scraper tools: they need her login and could get
her account closed.

## Free St. Joseph papers
- **State Historical Society of Missouri** (shsmo.org/collections/newspapers/mdnp, then "Search Digital
  Newspapers"), hosted free on Newspapers.com: St. Joseph Gazette 1879 to 1902, St. Joseph Herald 1865
  to 1877 and 1882 to 1900, Weekly Free Democrat 1859 to 1861, St. Joseph Journal 1939 to 1950.
- **Chronicling America** (Library of Congress), including the St. Joseph Observer 1906 to 1932. If the
  Library of Congress connector is connected, search it directly and give her links to the page scans.

## Copyright rule of thumb
In the US, anything published in 1930 or earlier is public domain. For newer clippings, share the
Newspapers.com clipping link or retell the story in her own words with a short quote. Not legal advice.

## Money then and now
When a story has a dollar amount, give the rough equivalent in today's money and say it is approximate.
Her readers always ask.
