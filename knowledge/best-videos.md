**Before St. Joseph Was a City: the Blacksnake Hills** (about 2 minutes; 48K Facebook views, 299 shares)
> Before St. Joseph was a city, before the Platte Purchase made it a part of Missouri, this land was called the Blacksnake Hills, and it wasn't America.
> It was a borderland caught between empires.
> Before there was a trading post, this land was the sovereign territory of the Iowa, Sac, and Fox tribes.
> The only white men who saw it were a few explorers, like Lewis and Clark, who passed by in 1805. [CORRECTION: July 1804, and again in September 1806]
> For centuries, this was native land.
> Then in 1826, a French-Canadian fur trader named Joseph Robidoux arrived.
> The American Fur Company hired him to establish a trading post at the Blacksnake Hills.
> This wasn't a town. It was a small cluster of rough buildings, a place to trade guns, beads and whiskey for furs.
> For a decade this post was the only real settlement here. Robidoux lived here with his employees, a few Frenchmen and his enslaved workers.
> Life was raw and dangerous.
> Then everything changed in 1836 when the U.S. government pressured the Iowa, Sac, and Fox tribes to sign away this land in the Platte Purchase for just $7,500.
> The treaty used careful language, claiming the tribes wanted to leave to avoid collisions with citizens.
> When the treaty was finalized in 1837, Robidoux's trading post was suddenly inside the United States.
> He platted the town of St. Joseph in 1843, naming it after his patron saint, Joseph.
> So when you stand at the south entrance of the Riverfront Park today, look at the river.
> Imagine it not as the edge of a city, but as the center of a borderland, a place where one world was ending and another was about to begin.
